# How radar sees Mexico City

Most satellites take pictures using sunlight. Radar sends out its own pulse of energy and measures what bounces back, so it works day or night, even through clouds. [NEEDS SOURCE ROW: NASA Earthdata "What is SAR", earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/what-is-sar]

NISAR uses L-band radar, with a wavelength of about 23 cm. [NASA Earthdata, earth-observation-data-basics/sar]

Radar waves can be sent and received in different directions, called polarizations: H for horizontal and V for vertical. [NEEDS SOURCE ROW: NASA Earthdata "What is SAR", earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/what-is-sar]

- **HH** is most sensitive to "double bounce," when the signal bounces off the ground and then off something upright, like a building. [NASA Earthdata, earth-observation-data-basics/sar]
- **HV** is most sensitive to "volume scattering," when the signal scatters around inside something like a tree canopy. [NASA Earthdata, earth-observation-data-basics/sar]

*We infer:* HH helps pick out the city's buildings, and HV helps pick out vegetation.
